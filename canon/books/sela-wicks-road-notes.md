Third crossing this month, and the fog by the old road still hasn't taught me
anything new. It stands thick on both sides like a wall, and never once have I
felt it stir — no wind, not even a breath of one, to push it back or thin it
out. If there's a wind blowing anywhere else in the world that day, it isn't
reaching this stretch of road.

And it's quiet the way an empty room is quiet, not the way a night is quiet.
No birds, no insects, no water running somewhere I can't see. The only sound
the whole way through is my own boots on the wet ground, over and over, until
I start listening for it just to have something to listen to.

— from the road-notes of Sela Wick, peddler
